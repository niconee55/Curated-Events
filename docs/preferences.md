## Quality Bar
- Favor high-signal, specific, upcoming events
- Avoid generic listings, weak keyword matches, and tribute acts
- Avoid low-effort listicles unless they lead to a primary source
- Prefer official event pages, venue pages, team sites, and trusted publications
- Do not pad the list with weak anime or culture picks just to reach the cap
- It is acceptable to return fewer than the cap if there are not enough strong matches
- Distinguish between in-person attendance events and watch/release events
- If an event requires attendance or tickets, include price information when available
- Do not surface routine domestic league games just because they involve Real Madrid or PSG
- Domestic league fixtures should usually only be included when the opponent is major, the context is unusually important, or the match clearly carries title-race or knockout-level significance
- For the Premier League specifically, the bar can be lower than for other domestic leagues because it is an explicitly followed competition
- For the Premier League specifically, err toward inclusion when the fixture is meaningfully important to the title race, European places, relegation fight, derby context, or a major-club matchup
- For the Premier League specifically, the threshold should be loose enough to keep a broader meaningful slate, including additional big-club matches and consequential run-in games that may not be the single biggest fixture of the month
- When the month is dense with strong soccer, it is better to include a few extra real games than to over-prune and miss something I would actually want on the calendar
- In the seed run, it is better to include a broader meaningful slate of EPL, adjacent sports, and useful culture items than to miss things I might have wanted.
- Major releases should be meaningful broad-interest releases that fit the taste profile, not overly niche or hyper-specific items
- When evaluating music, err toward inclusion for real official studio albums, deluxe editions with substantial new material, or other clearly meaningful release-day moments from strong-fit artists.
- For music, "strong-fit" should mean artists the user would plausibly already know, save, or intentionally try based on the current named lane. If the recommendation feels like a critic's-adjacent deep cut or a generational mismatch, it probably does not clear the bar.
- For adjacent sports outside the core soccer profile, the bar can be lower in the seed run if the event is a major tournament, late-stage knockout game, rivalry game, or otherwise a broadly compelling sporting moment
- For adjacent soccer competitions, lower the bar further once the event becomes a semifinal, final, or similarly decisive one-off match.
- For album releases, be broader and more inclusive than for expensive in-person concerts, because trying a strong-fit new album has lower downside than attending a costly show
- Missing a strong-fit official album release is worse than carrying one extra plausible release item into the report.
- The confidence threshold should be calibrated to “would I genuinely want this on my calendar,” not to “only a tiny handful of events survive”
- Calibrate the threshold a bit lower than before when the category is soccer, especially for Champions League, major tournaments, and meaningful EPL fixtures
- Calibrate the threshold a bit lower than before for useful Manhattan culture too, as long as the events are public-facing, not overly niche, and sensibly formatted for calendar use
- The `limit` is a ceiling, not a target to undershoot when many strong fits exist in the month
- When there are many plausible strong fits, err clearly toward inclusion rather than over-pruning
- The seed run should surface a wider slate of plausible calendar-worthy events, including adjacent sports I might get into, while the later update run can sharpen, prune, and add richer context
